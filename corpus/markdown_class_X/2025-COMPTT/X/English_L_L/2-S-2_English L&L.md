1.      Read the following passage carefully :                                            10

        (1) On a Sunday full of possibilities, I’m confused by too many choices. I
           unlock my phone and innumerable choices pour : movies, music,
           podcasts, games, and this truly perplexes me. Amidst my indecision, a
           dust-ridden copy of ‘The Bell Jar’ falls from the bookshelf. My guilt
           finds respite in the puddle of addictive videos online.

        (2) In a hyperconnected world, books are left to age pale yellow. Reading
           is seen by the newer generations as mundane. Though the benefits of
           reading are common knowledge, the privilege of choice overpowers.
           Many publishers and bookstores have now shut down.

        (3) ‘The blast of the digital’ has made everything catering to shrinking
           attention spans, with overwhelming choices from online poetry to
           captions to tweets. The reading fabric of Gen Z is brief. They are
           reading rapidly and recklessly. Reading for sole bliss seems to be a
           thing of the past. Though digital reading might be a new avenue for
           words,    studies   suggest     that   printed   text     develops    better
           comprehension.

        (4) The Reading Manifesto of October 2023 rightly argues that digital
           reading is ‘superficial’ and confined to mere text decoding. Reading
           text, on the other hand, helps cultivate better cognitive abilities,
           attention span and vocabulary. We live in an age where fake news
           prevails. The much required ability to distinguish between fact and
           fiction can only be developed by critical reading of printed texts.
