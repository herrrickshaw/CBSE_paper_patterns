     (1) Science fiction, often abbreviated as sci-fi, is a genre of literature and

         While sci-fi has long captivated audiences in western countries, its

         gradually gaining popularity among Indian readers and writers.



     (2) One of the major advantages of science fiction is its ability to stimulate
         imagination and curiosity. It encourages readers to think beyond the
         present and explore possibilities that lie in the future. For students and
         young readers, sci-fi can act as a bridge between literature and science,
         making learning more engaging. Books like Isaac Asimov’s Foundation
         or Indian writer Samit Basu’s Turbulence have introduced scientific
         ideas in exciting narratives.



     (3) Science fiction also reflects social and ethical concerns. In a rapidly
         changing world, sci-fi allows authors to speculate on how technology
         might shape society, for better or worse. In India, where issues like
         climate change, overpopulation, and digital surveillance are becoming
         more relevant, science fiction provides a creative lens through which
         these problems can be examined.

2.       Read the following passage carefully :                                         10

         (1) Introduction : Nutrition plays a critical role in human development,
            especially during periods of growth, aging, and recovery. This case
            study explores the changing nutritional requirements across age
            groups — from children to the elderly ⎯ highlighting how dietary
            habits must adapt to support health and wellbeing.

         (2) Methodology : A team of dietitians conducted a three-month
            observational study across five schools, two universities, three
            workplaces, and two retirement communities. Nutritional intake was
            recorded using food diaries, and health parameters were monitored
            through BMI, energy levels, and blood reports.

         (3) Survey Examination : Participants were grouped into four age
            categories : children (6 – 12 years), adolescents (13 – 19 years), adults
            (20 – 59 years), and elderly (60+ years). Surveys assessed daily intake
            of macronutrients (carbohydrates, proteins, fats) and micronutrients
            (vitamins and minerals), alongside hydration habits and meal
            frequency.

         (4) Results : Children had higher energy and protein needs per kilogram
            of body weight, essential for growth and cognitive development.
            Adolescents showed increased calorie and calcium requirements due
            to rapid bone growth and hormonal changes. Adults required
            balanced intake to maintain weight and prevent chronic diseases,
            while the elderly needed more fibre, calcium and vitamin D to
            support bone health and digestion. Interestingly, adolescents
            consumed the most processed foods, while elderly participants had
            the most balanced diets due to regular meal routines. Adults often
            skipped meals due to work stress. Dehydration was common in both
            children and the elderly.
